# **Key Terms & Definitions**

- **Transcript**
  - *Definition:* A written or typed representation of spoken language from an audio or video recording.
- **`Transcript not available`**
  - *Definition:* A metadata message indicating the text version of the recorded content is missing, inaccessible, or was not produced.
- **Audio Source**
  - *Definition:* The original recording (audio or video) from which a transcript would be created.
- **Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR)**
  - *Definition:* Software that converts spoken language into text automatically using machine learning.
- **Human Transcription**
  - *Definition:* A person manually listens to audio and types the spoken content into text, often used to improve accuracy over ASR.
- **Speaker Diarization**
  - *Definition:* The process of determining "who spoke when" in an audio file.
- **Timestamps**
  - *Definition:* Time markers inserted into a transcript that link text to specific moments in the recording.
- **Confidence Score**
  - *Definition:* A numeric estimate (usually from ASR) of how likely a transcribed word or sentence is correct.
- **Metadata**
  - *Definition:* Descriptive data about the recording or transcript (e.g., duration, speaker names, language, file ID, access permissions).
- **Data Completeness**
  - *Definition:* The extent to which required data (here, the transcript) exists and is available for use.
- **Gap Analysis**
  - *Definition:* The process of identifying missing information and planning how to fill those gaps.
- **Redaction**
  - *Definition:* The removal or masking of sensitive content from a transcript for privacy or legal reasons.
- **Provenance**
  - *Definition:* The history of a data item (who created it, when, and how), important for trust and reproducibility.

# **Core Principles & Best Practices (when a transcript is missing)**

- **Verify provenance and metadata**
  - Confirm whether the recording exists, who produced it, and why the transcript is missing.
- **Transparency**
  - Document that `Transcript not available` and record the cause (e.g., failed ASR, access restriction, intentionally redacted).
- **Prioritize privacy and legality**
  - Ensure you have rights/consent before requesting or transcribing recordings.
- **Assess alternatives**
  - Consider ASR, human transcription, speaker notes, or requesting original materials.
- **Quality control**
  - Use confidence scores, spot-checks, or human review to validate any generated transcript.
- **Document gap-filling steps**
  - Keep records of all actions taken to reconstruct or substitute missing content.

# **Practical Immediate Steps When You See `Transcript not available`**

- Check whether the original recording exists and is accessible.
- Inspect metadata and logs for error messages or notes.
- Confirm whether the absence is intentional (e.g., redaction) or accidental.
- If allowed, run ASR to generate a preliminary transcript; record the ASR confidence.
- If accuracy is critical, commission a human transcriber.
- If reconstruction is attempted using nearby chunks, mark inferred content clearly and avoid inventing exact words.
- Document all steps taken and the reason for any remaining gaps.
